## 2. What to say when they pick up

You are not shopping. You are opening a trade account. Say so in the first
sentence or you will be quoted the walk-in price.

> "Good morning. My name is Stephen, I run an online shop in Accra delivering
> across the city. I am looking for a regular supplier, not a one-off purchase.
> Do you sell to retailers at trade price?"

Then, in this order:

1. **"What is the carton price on [product]?"** Carton, not unit. Asking by the
   carton is the signal that you are trade rather than retail.
2. **"What is the minimum order?"** Nobody in Ghana publishes this. It is
   always a phone conversation. Expect to be told a carton quantity.
3. **"Do I need a registered business or a TIN to open an account?"**
4. **"What are your payment terms?"** Credit is normal in this chain. Ask on the
   second call, not the first.
5. **"Do you deliver, or do I collect?"** Delivery to your storage changes the
   real cost per unit.

**Write the answer down the same day.** Then put the dealer cost straight into
WooCommerce > Product Costs while you still remember which quote was which.

**On haggling.** Opening at 30 to 50 percent of the quoted price is documented
practice in Ghanaian markets, with achieved discounts of 50 to 63 percent.
UNVERIFIED whether wholesale tiers move as far as market stalls do, but the
first number is never the last number. Bundling several products into one order
moves it further than arguing about one.

## 6. Things that will cost you money if you do not know them

### Tip Toe Lane burned in January 2026

The Circle electronics market **burned on 20 January 2026**. Three weeks later
traders were still asking for help. No source covers its state after February
2026. Circle has also had repeated fires, demolitions and shop closures over
trader nationality disputes.

**Phone before you travel, and never single-source from Circle.**

### The certification regime changed on 1 July 2026

Ghana Standards Authority now issues Certificates of Conformity directly,
through the **EasyPass** portal. Bureau Veritas has stopped. Any guidance dated
2024 or 2025 is obsolete.

- **easypass@gsa.gov.gh**, 025 606 1679 / 025 606 1680
- Route B, product registration, is the right one for a repeating range: 0.500%
  plus USD 400 a year for 15 items, minimum USD 300.

Your electricals are in scope. GSA's high-risk list names electrical appliances,
bulbs, switches, sockets and cables explicitly.

**Cables and wiring accessories** need certification under L.I. 2478, and
enforcement reaches into shops, not just the port. Customs and Police can seize
stock from your storage. Wholesalers and retailers are required to verify
compliance before distributing.

**Fans need Energy Commission registration and an energy label.** A standing fan
sold to a household is a "comfort fan" under L.I. 2460. Register the model at
efficiency.energycom.gov.gh before you sell it.

Extension boards are almost certainly in scope under Chapter 85, but no source
names them explicitly. Ask GSA rather than assume.

### The substandard goods problem is real and it is at China Mall

On **19 May 2026** GSA sealed a section of the **Ashaiman China Mall warehouse**.
Of 15 bulbs sampled, all 15 failed. Bulbs sold as 15 watts measured **51 lumens
against a 360-lumen minimum**. The packaging was in Chinese, which is itself a
violation: English markings are mandatory and Chinese-only packaging has
triggered seizures in 2026.

That is the same branch listed above as the wholesale one. Buy from it with your
eyes open: check the actual output, insist on English packaging, and do not take
lighting stock on trust.

Ghana National Fire Service recorded **881 domestic fires in Q1 2026**, with
electrical faults the leading cause. If you sell a bad extension board and a
house burns, that is your name on it.

### Foreign-owned suppliers can be closed without warning

Retail in Ghana is legally reserved for Ghanaians; foreign traders may only
wholesale. That is *why* a Chinese or Nigerian supplier sells you cartons rather
than pieces. GUTA has repeatedly forced foreign-owned shops shut: Abossey Okai
in September 2025, over 200 shops in Kumasi in December 2025, Ashanti threats in
February 2026.

**A supplier you build a relationship with this month can be locked up next
month. Always have a second number for every product.**

### The port math, if you ever import directly

Roughly **48% of CIF** in duty and levies before handling, agent and conformity
fees, so a realistic landed cost of **1.4 to 1.6x CIF**. Duty band for finished
consumer goods is 20%; computers are usually 10%.

Two things changed recently and both help you:

- The COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy was **abolished on 1 January 2026**.
  VAT + NHIL + GETFund is now 20% effective, down from 21.9%.
- The **VAT registration threshold rose from GHS 200,000 to GHS 750,000**. You
  are probably below it. Check before registering.

One thing does not help you. The benchmark value discount was fully reversed in
January 2023, so duty is assessed on full value.

**Live risk:** in April 2026 GUTA alleged the new "Publican" AI valuation system
raised duties by up to 300%, and directed traders to stop paying. The strike was
suspended on 17 April pending consultation. **No resolution was found after
that.** Check the position before committing to any shipment.

**Conformity minimums make small trial imports uneconomic.** With a USD 250 to
300 minimum fee and an exemption only below USD 2,000 FOB, a small test
container costs more in certification than it saves in unit price. For your
volume, buying from an Accra importer who already holds the certificate is
almost certainly cheaper than importing yourself.

### You are late for back to school 2026

Basic schools open **8 September 2026**. Senior high opens **18 September 2026**.
Sea freight from China to Tema runs five to seven weeks plus clearing, so a
container ordered today will not land in time.

**This year:** buy local wholesale stock through August, at Kwashieman, and
accept the thinner margin. Be shelf-ready by about 20 August.
**Next year:** place the order in April or May 2027.

Free SHS does not cover school bags, so they stay a parent purchase. That is
demand-positive for you.

## 7. What could not be found

These are findings, not gaps in effort. Each one changes what you should do.

- **An official Silver Crest distributor for Ghana.** It appears to move through
  independent importers and market traders, not an appointed distributor. Which
  means Silver Crest is a walking job, not a phoning job.
- **Any published minimum order quantity, carton price or dealer tier, from any
  Ghanaian supplier.** Not one. This is the structure of the market, not a
  research failure: every dealer price is quote-on-phone. Budget time for
  negotiation rather than for comparison shopping.
- **A 2.5L electric deep fryer at any price in Ghana.** Smallest available is 3L.
- **A 4-port USB hub.** The market sells 7-port at GHS 29 and 8-in-1 USB-C at
  GHS 54.
- **A 4-socket board that states surge protection.** The 4-ways do not claim it;
  the ones that do are 6-socket.
- **A 20W USB-C charger or a 3-in-1 charging cable on any Ghanaian marketplace.**
- **An insulated lunch bag with a bottle.** Ghana bundles cutlery, not bottles.
- **An authorised Oraimo or New Age distributor for Ghana.** Oraimo's own "where
  to buy" page lists no physical stores at all.
- **The brand "Faster".** No Ghana presence found of any kind.
- **China Mall minimum orders or dealer terms.**
- **Melcom's trade counter policy.** Their site could not be read. Melcom appears
  to buy rather than wholesale to competitors.
- **HS-line duty rates** for 8509, 8516 and 7323. Have a clearing agent pull the
  actual ICUMS line per product.

## 8. Two things the prices are telling you

**Your listed prices are above the market on most of the catalogue.** Look down
the "market retail" column in section 3. Your GHS 280 Silver Crest 2L competes
with GHS 129. Your GHS 75 mouse competes with GHS 29. Your GHS 150 laptop stand
competes with GHS 23. Those are not dealer prices, they are what a customer can
already buy the same thing for today.

This is not a disaster: your prices were always placeholders, and a WhatsApp
shop with fast Accra delivery can sell above a marketplace listing. But the gap
on some lines is three to six times, and no amount of advertising fixes that.
**Reprice against this column before you spend a cedi on ads.**

**The brand distributors are the wrong channel for most of your catalogue.**
Silver Crest 2L retails at GHS 129 to 160. Binatone's 2L retails at GHS 1,400.
That is a ninefold spread on nominally the same product. Your catalogue sits in
the value tier, which means Interworld and Electroland are right for **irons,
kettles and the microwave** and wrong for everything else.

The one clear exception is worth a call on its own: **Electroland sells the Midea
20L microwave at GHS 699 retail, against GHS 950 on Jumia for the same SKU.**
That is 26% below market before you have negotiated anything.
