02What to say

You are not shopping, you are opening a trade account. Say so in the first sentence or you get quoted the walk-in price.

"Good morning. My name is Stephen, I run an online shop in Accra delivering across the city. I am looking for a regular supplier, not a one-off purchase. Do you sell to retailers at trade price?"

Then, in this order:

  1. "What is the carton price on [product]?" Carton, not unit. Asking by the carton is the signal that you are trade.
  2. "What is the minimum order?" Nobody in Ghana publishes this. It is always a phone conversation.
  3. "Do I need a registered business or a TIN to open an account?"
  4. "What are your payment terms?" Credit is normal in this chain. Ask on the second call, not the first.
  5. "Do you deliver, or do I collect?" Delivery changes the real cost per unit.

Write it down the same day, then put the dealer cost into WooCommerce › Product Costs while you still remember which quote was which.

On haggling: opening at 30 to 50 percent of the quoted price is documented practice in Ghanaian markets, with achieved discounts of 50 to 63 percent. The first number is never the last number, and bundling several products into one order moves it further than arguing about one.

06What will cost you money if you do not know it

Tip Toe Lane burned on 20 January 2026. Three weeks later traders were still asking for help, and no source covers its state after February 2026. Circle has had repeated fires, demolitions and closures over trader nationality disputes. Phone before you travel, and never single-source from Circle.

Substandard goods, at the exact branch on your list. On 19 May 2026 Ghana Standards Authority sealed part of the Ashaiman China Mall warehouse. Of 15 bulbs sampled, all 15 failed: bulbs sold as 15 watts measured 51 lumens against a 360-lumen minimum, in Chinese-only packaging. English markings are mandatory and Chinese-only packaging has triggered seizures this year.

Buy there with your eyes open. Check the actual output and insist on English packaging. Ghana National Fire Service recorded 881 domestic fires in the first quarter of 2026, with electrical faults the leading cause. If you sell a bad extension board and a house burns, that is your name on it.

The certification rules changed on 1 July 2026

Ghana Standards Authority now issues Certificates of Conformity directly, through the EasyPass portal. Bureau Veritas has stopped. Any guidance dated 2024 or 2025 is out of date.

easypass@gsa.gov.gh · 025 606 1679 / 025 606 1680. Route B, product registration, fits a repeating range: 0.5 percent plus USD 400 a year for 15 items.

Your electricals are in scope. Cables and wiring accessories need certification under L.I. 2478, and enforcement reaches into shops, not just the port: Customs and Police can seize stock from your storage, and retailers are required to verify compliance before distributing. Fans need Energy Commission registration and an energy label, at efficiency.energycom.gov.gh.

Foreign-owned suppliers can be closed without warning

Retail is legally reserved for Ghanaians; foreign traders may only wholesale. That is why a Chinese or Nigerian supplier sells you cartons rather than pieces. GUTA has repeatedly forced foreign-owned shops shut: Abossey Okai in September 2025, over 200 shops in Kumasi in December 2025, Ashanti threats in February 2026. Always have a second number for every product.

If you ever import directly

Roughly 48 percent of CIF in duty and levies before handling and agent fees, so a landed cost of about 1.4 to 1.6 times CIF. Two recent changes help: the COVID-19 Health Recovery Levy was abolished on 1 January 2026, and the VAT registration threshold rose from GHS 200,000 to GHS 750,000, so you are probably below it.

But conformity minimums make small trial imports uneconomic: a USD 250 to 300 minimum fee, with exemption only below USD 2,000 FOB. At your volume, buying from an Accra importer who already holds the certificate is almost certainly cheaper than importing yourself.

Live risk: in April 2026 GUTA alleged the new "Publican" AI valuation system raised duties by up to 300 percent and told traders to stop paying. The strike was suspended on 17 April pending consultation, and no resolution was found after that. Check before committing to a shipment.

You are late for back to school 2026

Basic schools open 8 September, senior high 18 September. Sea freight from China runs five to seven weeks plus clearing, so a container ordered today will not land in time. Buy local wholesale stock at Kwashieman through August and be shelf-ready by about 20 August. Place the container order in April or May 2027. Free SHS does not cover school bags, so they stay a parent purchase.

07What could not be found

These are findings, not gaps in effort. Each one changes what you should do.

  • An official Silver Crest distributor for Ghana. It moves through independent importers and market traders. Silver Crest is a walking job, not a phoning job.
  • Any published minimum order, carton price or dealer tier, from any Ghanaian supplier. Not one. Every dealer price is quote-on-phone. That is the market's structure, not a research failure.
  • A 2.5L deep fryer at any price in Ghana. Smallest available is 3L.
  • A 4-port USB hub, a 4-socket board stating surge protection, a 20W USB-C charger, a 3-in-1 charging cable, and an insulated lunch bag with a bottle. None found on any Ghanaian marketplace.
  • An authorised Oraimo or New Age distributor for Ghana. Oraimo's own "where to buy" page lists no physical stores at all.
  • The brand "Faster". No Ghana presence of any kind.
  • China Mall minimum orders or dealer terms, and Melcom's trade counter policy.

08Two things the prices are telling you

Your listed prices are above the market on most of the catalogue, and on some lines by three to six times.

Your GHS 280 blender competes with GHS 129. Your GHS 75 mouse competes with GHS 29. Your GHS 150 laptop stand competes with GHS 23. Those are not dealer prices, they are what a customer can already buy the same thing for today.

This is not a disaster. Your prices were always placeholders, and a WhatsApp shop with fast Accra delivery genuinely sells above a marketplace listing. But no amount of advertising fixes a six-times gap. Reprice against that column before you spend a cedi on ads.

You are also under market on several: earbuds, ring lights, drying racks, shavers and the solar fan. Those are the ones to push first.

The other lesson is which door to knock on. Silver Crest 2L retails at GHS 129 to 160; Binatone's 2L retails at GHS 1,400. A ninefold spread on nominally the same product. Your catalogue sits in the value tier, so Interworld and Electroland are right for irons, kettles and the microwave and wrong for most of the rest.

One exception is worth the call on its own. Electroland sells the Midea 20L microwave at GHS 699 retail, against GHS 950 on Jumia for the same unit. That is 26 percent below market before you have negotiated anything.
