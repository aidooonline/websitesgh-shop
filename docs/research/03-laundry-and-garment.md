# Research: irons and garment care in Ghana

Date: 24 July 2026. Figures computed from the verified PURC rate (GHS 2.04/kWh,
residential 0 to 300 kWh, from 1 July 2026).

## The angle
Irons are heat appliances, and heat is where the electricity bill lives. Nobody
selling irons in Ghana talks about running cost. We do, and it drives the choice
between dry and steam.

| Iron | Draw | 20 min/day, GHS/month | GHS/year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry iron 1200W | 1200W | **24.45** | 293 |
| Steam iron 1450W | 1450W | **29.54** | 354 |
| Garment steamer 1000W | 1000W (10 min) | **10.19** | 122 |

Selling insight: the steamer is the cheapest to run because sessions are short,
and it is the upsell for shirts-only customers. The dry iron is the value play.
The steam iron earns its extra GHS 5 a month only if the buyer irons cottons.

## Buyer questions
1. Dry iron or steam iron, which for Ghana? (Steam for cotton and kaba, dry for
   uniforms and polyester blends, steamer for office shirts.)
2. Why does my iron trip the meter? (2000W+ on a loaded circuit. Explain amps.)
3. Non stick vs stainless sole plate? (Non stick glides on synthetics, stainless
   lasts longer on heavy cotton.)
